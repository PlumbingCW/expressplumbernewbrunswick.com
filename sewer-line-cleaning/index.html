Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in New Brunswick, NJ

Sewage backing up into a tub, floor drain or basement is not something to sit on. Call (732) 466-1098 and we will talk you through the first steps while a plumber is sent.

(732) 466-1098

Sewage is backing up right now

A main line backup is urgent because every drain in the house feeds the same blocked pipe. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, and calling gets a plumber moving. While you wait, stop all water use, keep everyone out of the affected rooms and leave the washer and dishwasher off.

Gurgling floor drains, toilets that bubble when you run the sink, and wastewater rising in the lowest tub or shower are the usual signs of a blocked main. Basement floor drains show it first because they sit lowest on the line.

If the backup has reached outlets, a furnace or the panel, stay away from them. Shut power off only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water.

  • Do not flush toilets or run any tap.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing wastewater.
  • Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must cross wet floors.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a main line backup, they sit in the water and burn the next person who works on it.

Sewer backup: what to do first

  1. Stop adding waterTurn off the dishwasher and washer, hold off on showers, and ask everyone in the house to stop flushing. Each gallon you send down has nowhere to go but back up.
  2. Find the lowest drainNote which fixtures are affected. If only one sink is slow, it may be a branch clog. If the basement floor drain and the first-floor toilet both act up, the main is blocked.
  3. Locate the cleanoutLook for a capped pipe, often 3 or 4 inches wide, near where the main leaves the house. Leave the cap on unless a plumber tells you otherwise, because opening it under pressure can release a surge.
  4. Call and describe what you seeTell the dispatcher what is overflowing, how fast, and whether anyone has used water in the last hour. It helps us decide what equipment to bring.
  5. Protect what you canLift rugs, move boxes off the floor and open windows for air. Photograph any damage for your insurer before cleanup.

Where the main line runs and where the cleanout is

The main sewer line carries everything from your house to the connection in the street or easement. Most homes have a cleanout, a capped access pipe, either in the basement near the foundation wall, in a crawlspace, or outside near the building. It gives a cleaning cable or jetter a straight way into the line.

If you cannot find one, say so when you call. A house without a reachable cleanout is usually cleaned through a toilet or a roof vent, which takes more care and more time. Adding a cleanout is often a sensible follow-up, and it is covered under plumbing installation.

According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board, only a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor may perform plumbing in the state. A main line cleaning that involves opening the cleanout or touching house piping falls under that rule, so confirm who will be on site.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

What actually clogs a main line

Tree roots

Fine roots find joints and cracks and then thicken into a net that catches paper. This tends to repeat, because the roots grow back unless the joint is repaired.

Grease and soap

Kitchen fat cools in the pipe and builds up as a layer. It narrows the bore until solids stop moving.

Wipes and hygiene products

Products labeled flushable often do not break down. They wrap around roots or rough spots and form plugs.

Pipe damage

A sag, an offset joint, or a collapsed section holds water and debris. Cleaning clears it for a while, but only a repair fixes it.

Who owns which part of the line

In general the pipe from your house to the property line is the owner's responsibility, and the main in the street belongs to the sewer operator. New Brunswick sewer operation and any combined sewer details are not something we can state here, so ask the city or your sewer operator who maintains the main on your street.

In Highland Park, the borough's water and wastewater system is run by Utility Service Affiliates, a Middlesex Water subsidiary, so a backup in the street main there goes to that operator. If you live near the border, our Highland Park service page explains how we handle calls there.

Source: Middlesex Water, Utility Service Affiliates Highland Park page

How we judge whether it will come back

After the line runs clear, we look at what came out. Roots on the cable point to a joint problem, while grease and paper point to habits or a rough spot. How fast the line backed up last time also matters, since a clog that returns within weeks is rarely just a clog.

If the camera shows a broken or sagging pipe, cleaning is a stopgap and the next step is sewer line repair. If the pipe is sound, regular cleaning and a few kitchen habits are usually enough.
