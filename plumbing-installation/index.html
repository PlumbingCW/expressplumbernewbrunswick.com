Plumbing Installation in New Brunswick, NJ

New bathroom, added kitchen, basement fixtures or a new run of pipe. Call (732) 466-1098 to plan it, and see water heater installation if a new heater is part of the job.

Before the first pipe is cut

Installation is a planned job, so the best time to call is early, while walls are still open on paper. Tell us which rooms are changing, what fixtures you have picked and where the existing stack, main and water lines run. A short phone call is enough to start, and we explain the scope and give a clear price before work starts.

Licensed and insured, we serve New Brunswick and nearby towns. If the job needs a permit, we help you understand what to ask for. The permit question is below, in plain terms.

Rough-in and finish

  1. Plan and layoutWe confirm fixture locations, wall depth, venting and where drains need to fall.
  2. Rough-inPipes are set inside open walls and floors before drywall. Drain slope and vent paths are fixed here.
  3. Inspection stageIf your job needs one, inspection happens with the pipes still visible. Leave walls open until it passes.
  4. Close up and finishAfter walls are closed, we set the fixtures, connect the valves and test for leaks.
  5. WalkthroughWe show you the shutoff valves and how to use them.

Basement bathrooms

Below-grade rooms need extra planning because drains cannot fall to a sewer that sits higher than the floor. Often the answer is a sewage ejector, a sealed basin with a pump that lifts waste up to the main drain. It needs a vent, a check valve and a power supply that stays on.

A concrete floor must be cut to bring drains in. Plan for the dust and for the time to cure patch concrete before tile goes down. A sump pit is not an ejector pit, and mixing them up causes trouble. If groundwater is already a worry, see basement waterproofing first.

Permits and inspections

New plumbing or a change to existing drains and vents often falls under a permit, but we will not state rules we cannot source. Ask the New Brunswick building department what your project needs and whether a licensed plumber must pull the permit.

Tell us if the permit is already in hand when you call. Keep the inspection sign-off with your house records, because buyers and insurers sometimes ask for it later.

According to the state Department of Community Affairs code alert, installing additional new fixtures in an existing space is minor work in dwellings when the water distribution system does not grow in size. A like-for-like swap with no piping change needs no permit, so the line between the two matters.

Source: NJ Department of Community Affairs code alert on 2018 Uniform Construction Code changes, nj.gov/dca/codes/alerts/pdfs/OM_MW_03052018.pdf

Fitting new work into an old house

Walls in older homes can hide old galvanized or cast-iron pipes. New fixtures on tired pipe can show low pressure fast, because the old line has narrowed inside. We check what you have before tying in, and we say when repiping part of the house is the saner option.

If you want to see how that decision plays out, read about repiping. A new bathroom on a failing line is money spent twice.

Edison and East Brunswick projects

Our reach includes Edison, with ZIPs including 08817 and 08820, and East Brunswick, ZIP 08816. Both are close to New Brunswick, so the same crew can often cover a job across the town line.

Permit offices differ from town to town, so check with the building department in the town where the work will be done. The Edison page and the East Brunswick page have more on those areas.

What drives the price of an install

Homeowners insurance generally does not pay for new work you choose to add. It may cover resulting damage if a new connection fails suddenly, so ask your insurer how your policy treats this.

We give a real number by phone once we know the rooms, the fixtures and the access. Call (732) 466-1098.

Fixtures chosenFixtures you supply or choose change the line items. Specialty valves cost more.
Access and floorsOpen framing is quick. Concrete cutting, tight crawl spaces and finished ceilings add time.
Drain and vent changesNew drain routes, vents or an ejector add parts and labour.

What to have ready

  • Fixture model numbers or a clear photo of what you chose.
  • A sketch of the room, even rough, with sizes.
  • Where the main shutoff and the floor drain are.
  • Permit details or the name of the contact you spoke to.
  • Photos of any pipes you can see in the basement or crawl space.
