Emergency Drain Cleaning in New Brunswick, NJ

Water rising in a sink, tub or floor drain needs a fast answer. Call (732) 466-1098 any hour and we will sort out whether it is a simple clog or a sewer line cleaning job.

Water coming back up: do this first

A drain that is overflowing or pushing water back up cannot wait for the weekend, and our dispatch line runs 24/7 for exactly this. While you wait for the call to connect and a technician to be scheduled, stop using every water source in the house. That means no flushing, no dishwasher, no washing machine and no showers.

If the water is coming from a basement floor drain or a toilet, move belongings off the floor and keep children and pets away. Dirty backup water carries bacteria, so wear rubber boots and gloves if you have to walk through it.

  • Do not pour chemical cleaner into a drain that is already full. It sits in the water and can splash back on you.
  • Unplug anything electric near standing water, and do not touch outlets or appliances while you are wet.
  • Switch off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. Otherwise leave it and say so on the call.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.

Kitchen sink and tub clogs

Kitchen sinks clog because of what goes down them over months. Grease cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, then coffee grounds, rice and food scraps catch on that coating until the pipe is nearly closed. The sink drains a little slower each week, then stops.

Tubs and showers clog higher up. Hair wraps around the stopper or the first bend, then soap scum holds it in place. Water pooling at your ankles points to a blockage near the opening.

Kitchen sink

Grease and food buildup in the trap and the first run of pipe. Often clears with a hand-fed cable.

Bathroom sink

Hair and toothpaste residue gather at the pop-up stopper and in the trap below it.

Tub or shower

Hair knots at the strainer or the first elbow. Slow draining and a faint odor are common signs.

Laundry standpipe

Lint and detergent sludge build up until the standpipe overflows.

When a clog is really a main line problem

One slow sink points to a local blockage. Several slow fixtures point further down the line. If the toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or a floor drain bubbles when you run the shower, the air and water have nowhere to go because the main drain is restricted.

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are involved and in what order they act up. That pattern saves time on site, because it tells the technician whether to start at a fixture or at the main cleanout.

Snaking or jetting

A drain cable, often called a snake, bores through a blockage or hooks it and pulls it out. It is the right tool for hair, a soft food clog or a small obstruction.

Hydro-jetting uses pressurized water to scour the pipe wall, so it suits heavy grease, scale and roots in a larger line. It costs more and is not needed for every job. We start with the lighter tool when it fits and explain the reason if we move up. Read more about hydro-jetting if the same line keeps slowing down.

According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board, the work of opening and repairing drain lines belongs to a licensed master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Employees reported on a W-2 may also do plumbing work. Ask which of those applies before anyone starts.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

Why we go easy on chemical cleaners

Store-bought liquid cleaners are caustic. They can sit in a stopped pipe for hours, soften old fittings and burn skin if the line is cleared by hand afterward. They also rarely clear hair or a solid obstruction, they only thin some of the grease.

If you already poured some in, say so when you call. The technician will wear protective gear and handle the trap with care. A plain plunger and a bucket of hot tap water are safer first tries on a sink that is still draining slowly.

Sewer lines, basements and who is responsible

If the blockage is past your property line, the sewer main belongs to the utility, not to you. In nearby Highland Park, for example, the borough's water and wastewater system is operated by Utility Service Affiliates, a Middlesex Water subsidiary, so a main-line backup there is a call to that operator after a plumber confirms your own line is clear. Our Highland Park page covers that town.

We do not have a sourced answer on who runs the sewer under your New Brunswick street. Ask the city's public works office, and the same question applies in North Brunswick. A technician can check that your own pipe is clear so you can tell the utility what you found.

Source: Middlesex Water, Utility Service Affiliates (Highland Park) page.

Tool neededA hand cable, a machine cable or a jetter each carry different time and equipment costs.
AccessA clear cleanout is easy. A line with no cleanout may need a fixture pulled.
Time of callNight, weekend and holiday visits are priced differently from a daytime appointment.
Repeat blockagesA line that clogs often may need a camera look to find the cause.
