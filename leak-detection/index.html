Emergency Leak Detection in New Brunswick, NJ

A wet ceiling, a meter that never stops or a bill that jumped all point to water going somewhere it should not. We find where it is leaving the pipe before anyone opens a wall.

(732) 466-1098

Water is actively leaking right now

If water is dripping from a ceiling, spreading across a floor or running somewhere you cannot see, act first and diagnose later. Dispatch answers 24/7, and while you wait the most useful thing you can do is close the main water valve by turning it clockwise until it stops.

Then open one low faucet to drain the pipes. If water is near outlets, light fixtures or appliances, switch off the breaker for that area and stay out of any standing water. Never touch electrical items while standing in water. A gas smell means leave and call the gas utility or 911 first.

  • Locate the main shutoff before you need it.
  • Move valuables and rugs out of the wet zone.
  • Put a bucket under ceiling drips.
  • Take photos of the damage for your insurer.

Meter still running or a high water bill

The water meter is the quickest honest test for a leak you cannot see. Turn off every tap, appliance and ice maker in the house. Then look at the meter. Many meters have a small low-flow indicator, often a dial or triangle, that spins when any water moves through.

If it keeps moving with everything off, water is going somewhere. Close the main valve inside the house and look again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the building. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house, and water line repair may be the next step.

Meter designs vary, so ask your water utility how to read yours. Toilets are a common cause of a quiet bill rise. Food coloring in the tank shows it if color reaches the bowl without flushing.

Wet ceiling or wall

A ceiling stain can come from a supply line, a drain, a roof or condensation, and those need different fixes. If the stain grows even when no water is running upstairs, suspect the roof or a pressurised line. If it grows after a shower or flush, suspect a drain or a tub seal.

We use a moisture meter and, where useful, a thermal camera to map how far water has spread before cutting anything. If the answer is the roof or exterior, we say so and point you to the right trade.

Slab, crawlspace and under-floor leaks

Leaks under a concrete floor or in a crawlspace show up as hot spots, a constant sound of running water, or a wet patch with no source above. We do not assume how your home is built. Foundations vary, so we check each one.

Acoustic listening gear picks up the hiss of pressurised water through concrete. Tracer gas, a harmless mix pushed into an emptied line, can confirm the location when sound is unclear. Only then do we talk about breaking the floor or rerouting the line.

A leak in the yard or between meter and house

Soft ground, a patch of grass that stays green or a sinkhole forming near the line are signs of a buried leak. So is low pressure all through the house. Water may travel along the pipe trench before it surfaces, so the wet spot is not always above the break.

Acoustic detection traces the pipe and finds where the leak noise is loudest, which keeps digging small.

According to New Jersey American Water, the company maintains the service line from the main to the curb stop, and the customer maintains the pipe from the curb stop to the building. A leak past the curb is generally the homeowner's repair, and the curb stop itself should be left to the utility.

Methods we use to find the source

Acoustic listening

Sensitive microphones on pipes, floors and valves pick up the sound of escaping water.

Thermal imaging

Shows temperature differences from hot lines or wet material behind surfaces.

Moisture meters

Map how far water has moved into drywall, wood and subfloor.

Tracer gas

Pinpoints leaks in pipes that give little sound, such as plastic or deep lines.

Businesses and multi-unit buildings

A leak in a shop or an apartment building often involves several tenants and a shared riser, so finding the source also means finding which unit it comes from. We trace it with the same tools, then hand the owner a written location.
