No heat and the house is getting cold

A dead boiler in winter is urgent, and our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait, dress in layers, close doors to keep heat in the rooms you use, and check that the thermostat is set to heat with fresh batteries if it takes them. Do not keep pressing the reset button, because repeated resets can flood a burner with unburned fuel.

If you smell gas, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the building at once and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not look for the cause first. Never run a gas oven or a portable fuel heater indoors to warm the house.

  • Look for water on the floor around the boiler and shut the feed valve if it is leaking.
  • Check the breaker or switch for the boiler and the fuel supply valve.
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas.
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls if pipes could freeze.

Leaks at the boiler

Water on the floor under a boiler can come from a loose valve packing, a weeping fitting, a relief valve that has discharged, or a failed section of the unit itself. The source matters, because a drip at a union is a small repair and a leak from the vessel is not.

Switch off the power and fuel to the boiler, close its water feed, and put a pan or towels down. If you cannot tell where the water is coming from, our leak detection page covers how we trace it when pipes run behind walls.

Bleeding radiators safely

  1. Turn the heat offLet the system cool so you are not opening a valve on hot water.
  2. Find the bleed valveOn a hot-water radiator it is a small valve near the top of one end. Steam radiators work differently and are not bled this way.
  3. Open slowly with a keyHold a rag below it and turn a quarter turn. Air hisses out first, then water.
  4. Close when water flowsTighten the valve as soon as a steady stream appears, then check the boiler pressure gauge and the feed.

Hot water, steam and cast iron radiators

Hot-water systems

A pump circulates heated water to radiators or baseboards. Pressure and trapped air are the usual troubles.

Steam systems

Steam rises to radiators through pipes and vents. They run at low pressure and fail differently, so do not bleed them like a hot-water system.

Cast iron radiators

They are heavy, long-lived and hold heat, but they can leak at valves and stall when air is trapped.

A technician identifies which system you have on site. Pictures of the boiler nameplate and the gauge help us prepare on the phone.

What a plumber handles and what needs a heating contractor

We handle the water side of a boiler. That includes leaks, valves, feed lines, expansion tanks, circulator connections and bleeding air. Burner, ignition and combustion work is a heating contractor's trade, and gas appliance work has its own rules.

If your problem turns out to be on the burner or gas side, we will say so on the phone or on site rather than guess. A new system or a replaced unit connected to existing piping falls under plumbing installation.

Boiler work in New Jersey still falls under the plumbing trade where pipes are involved. According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board FAQ, plumbing may only be performed by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor, so ask who is doing the pipe work before you agree to it.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

Renting and no heat

Tenants should tell the landlord or building manager the same day heat fails and keep a record of when. Rules on heat in rentals exist at the state and local level, and we cannot state them for you here. Ask your municipality or a tenant resource what applies to your building.
