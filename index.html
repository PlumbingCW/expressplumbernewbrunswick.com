What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

Most plumbing emergencies get worse every minute the water keeps moving. Before you call, take the steps below. They limit damage while you wait, and they help the dispatcher understand what you are dealing with.

Call the line at (732) 466-1098 at any hour. Describe where the water is coming from, which fixtures are affected, and whether anything electrical is near it. That short description lets a plumber plan the visit before arriving.

  1. Stop the waterFind the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line enters the house near the meter or in the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Protect the powerIf water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, shut off power to that area at the breaker. Never step into standing water near anything electrical.
  3. Stop using the drainsFor a backup, do not flush, run the washer or use the shower. Every drop you add has to go somewhere.
  4. Move what you canLift rugs, boxes and furniture away from the wet area. Photos of the damage are useful later if you decide to talk to your insurer.

Problems we are called for most

Clogged and slow drains lead the list. A single sink that drains slowly is usually a local clog, and drain cleaning clears it. When a tub, a toilet and a floor drain all misbehave at once, the blockage is usually in the main line, and sewer line cleaning or a camera inspection finds the cause.

Water heaters are the next big call. A tank that makes no hot water, drips at the base or rumbles when it heats can often be fixed through water heater repair. When the tank itself has failed, water heater replacement gets hot water back the same day when the schedule allows.

Leaks are the quiet problem. A wet ceiling, a damp wall or a water meter that keeps turning with every tap closed points to a hidden pipe. Leak detection traces it before the repair opens more wall than needed, and burst pipe repair handles the loud version.

  • Slow or backed-up drains, toilets and floor drains
  • No hot water, leaking tanks and noisy heaters
  • Hidden leaks, high water bills and wet walls
  • Burst, frozen or corroded pipes
  • Failed sump pumps and wet basements
  • Cold radiators and boiler faults

How a call becomes a repair

The process is short on purpose. You should never have to guess what comes next.

  1. You callA dispatcher asks what is happening, where it is and whether the water is stopped.
  2. A plumber is scheduledYou get the next available slot. Same-day appointments are offered when they are available.
  3. The problem is diagnosedThe plumber looks at the cause, not only the symptom, and explains the options in plain words.
  4. You hear the priceA clear price is given before work starts. Work begins only after you agree to it.

Older buildings, newer buildings, different risks

Only about 21 percent of New Brunswick housing units are owner-occupied, according to Census data for 2020 to 2024, so most calls involve a tenant and a landlord. If you rent, say so on the phone. Approval for a repair may have to come from the owner, and a plumber can tell you what to ask for.

New Brunswick mixes multifamily buildings near the university, single-family streets and commercial blocks downtown, and the plumbing in each behaves differently. Older cast iron and galvanized lines tend to clog and corrode, while newer plastic and PEX lines fail at fittings and connections.

That is why a phone description matters. A plumber who hears that you are in a basement apartment, a rowhouse or a restaurant kitchen can bring the right parts. For shops, offices and multifamily buildings, commercial plumbing service runs after hours too.

Planned work is scheduled, not rushed

Not every job is an emergency. A tired water heater that still works, a bathroom remodel or a house full of old pipe can be planned.
