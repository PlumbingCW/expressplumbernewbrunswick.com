A business district, not a subdivision

Downtown New Brunswick is a mixed-use district rather than a residential subdivision. It includes Civic Square, which holds City Hall, the library and the main post office. High-rise apartments have been added near the New Brunswick station since the turn of the century, and the old Hiram Market area was cleared for a Hyatt hotel and conference center.

This page is for the businesses and buildings in that district. For houses and apartments in the surrounding neighborhoods, see our pages for Esperanza and the Livingston Avenue Historic District.

Source: Wikipedia, New Brunswick, New Jersey

Restaurant kitchens along George Street

George Street is the main downtown thoroughfare. The city has closed it to traffic for a summer pedestrian plaza, and restaurants have used sidewalks and streets for dining starting in 2020. A busy dining district means busy kitchens.

Grease, food waste and wipes are the usual drain enemies in a commercial kitchen. A hydro jetting pass scours a line more thoroughly than a snake, and a drain cleaning visit is the first step for a slow floor drain. Ask the city or the sewer operator whether your kitchen needs a grease interceptor.

Source: newbrunswick.com, George Street pedestrian plaza report

Tall buildings and shared systems

In a multi-story building, a leak on one floor can show up two floors down. Report stains early, and ask the building manager which valves isolate your floor. A burst pipe repair call is easier when someone knows where to shut off the section.

For hot water in a multi-tenant building, find out whether the water heater is shared. If it is, the building owner usually has to approve water heater repair or a swap.

Planning a kitchen or retail build-out

  1. Map the drainsAsk the landlord for any plans that show floor drains, cleanouts and the main line before you place equipment.
  2. Check hot water capacityDishwashers, prep sinks and handwashing all draw from the same heater. Tell the plumber what will run at once.
  3. Ask the city about permitsAsk the building department what permits and inspections apply to new fixtures, and who files them.
  4. Schedule around opening dayBook rough-in work early so that inspections and finish work do not collide with your opening.

Our plumbing installation service covers new fixtures and lines for this kind of project.

Landlord, tenant and manager roles

In commercial space, the lease decides who pays. Some leases make the tenant responsible for fixtures inside the unit, while the owner handles shared lines. Check the lease or ask the property manager before approving work.

When you call, give the business name, the floor and the contact for the manager. We can explain the repair in plain terms so you can pass it on.

After-hours and planned work

  • Ask by phone how we handle evening or weekend calls for your building.
  • Plan larger jobs such as a kitchen build-out for slow hours.
  • Keep a plan of shutoff valves where staff can find it.
