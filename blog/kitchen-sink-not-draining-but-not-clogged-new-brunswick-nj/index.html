Kitchen Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged? What It Means in New Brunswick, NJ

A sink that drains slowly with nothing visibly stuck is telling you something specific. Here is how to read the signs, what is safe to try yourself, and when the cause sits further down the pipe.

By the New Brunswick Express Plumbing team

(732) 466-1098

Water sitting in the basin and no obvious plug

You pull the stopper, look down the drain, and see nothing. No food, no hair, no grease plug. Yet the basin takes a full minute to empty and the last inch of gray water lingers. Plumbers hear this one all the time, and the answer is rarely at the strainer.

A drain pipe does not need to be fully blocked to misbehave. A coating of grease and soap film can narrow a pipe until it carries only a trickle. A pipe with a weak vent can also hold water back, because air has no way in behind the water that is trying to leave.

Kitchen lines carry more fat and food residue than any other drain in the house, so partial blockages build quietly there. The sink can seem fine for months and then slow down all at once when the narrowed section catches a few coffee grounds or a bit of rice.

Reading the symptoms: which cause fits your sink

What you seeLikely causeFirst safe check
Slow drain in one bowl of a double sinkPartial blockage in the trap or tailpiece on that sideEmpty the trap into a bucket and look for sludge
Gurgling after the water leavesA restricted vent pulling air through the trapListen for gurgling at a second drain when this one empties
Water rises in the second bowl when the disposal runsDisposal chamber or the pipe just past the branch is partly blockedSwitch the disposal off at the wall and look for jammed waste
Dishwasher water shows up in the sinkShared drain narrowed beyond the dishwasher connectionCheck the hose loop and the disposal inlet plug
Sink, tub and toilet all slowBuildup in a shared branch or the main lineRun the nearest tub and see whether it also lingers

Treat the table as a starting point and not a diagnosis. Two causes can overlap, and a sink that gurgles and drains slowly may have both a narrowed pipe and a vent problem.

Someone who offers to open a wall or replace a drain line should be able to say who is licensed, because according to the state plumbing board FAQ, plumbing in New Jersey is limited to master plumbers and authorized contractors.

Apartment residents face a different question. The drain behind a kitchen wall may be shared with a neighbor, so tell your landlord or property manager before anyone opens a shared line. A backup that reaches another unit becomes a building problem, and early notice keeps it from spreading.

Safe checks you can do before anyone visits

  1. Stop adding waterDo not run the dishwasher or refill the basin while it stands full. More water only pushes dirty water toward the edges and into the cabinet below.
  2. Switch the disposal offIf you have a disposal, turn it off at the wall switch before you look near it. Never put a hand into a disposal chamber, even one you believe is off.
  3. Look under the cabinetCheck the trap and pipes for damp spots, drips or a sour smell. A wet cabinet floor means a leak as well as a slow drain, so close the shutoff valves under the sink and tell the dispatcher.
  4. Empty the trapSet a bucket under the U-shaped trap, loosen the slip nuts by hand and let it drain. Refit the washers flat, tighten by hand, and test with a small amount of water while you watch the joints.
  5. Plunge with the second drain blockedPress a wet rag into the other bowl or the overflow, then plunge with steady strokes. Skip chemical drain openers. They sit in standing water, can burn skin, and are hard on older pipe.

When the trouble sits past the sink

If the trap is clean and the sink still crawls, the narrowing is likely in the horizontal run inside the wall or floor. A hand tool cannot reach it, and a plunger only moves the water back and forth. This is the job that professional drain cleaning is built for, using a cable machine or, for heavy coating, a water-driven tool.

A disposal that hums, stalls or empties slowly can be part of the picture. Worn or jammed grinders hold waste in the chamber and slow everything behind them, and a technician who does garbage disposal repair can tell you whether the unit or the pipe is the real culprit.

Watch for signs that the trouble is bigger than one sink. Other drains gurgling, a toilet bubbling when the sink empties, or water appearing at a basement floor drain all suggest a shared line. In that case the kitchen sink is only the first fixture to complain.

The state plumbing board says, in its consumer FAQ, that New Jersey plumbing shall be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. A problem in the branch line or stack behind the wall sits squarely in that territory, so it is a call rather than a weekend project.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

What to tell the dispatcher when you call

  • Which sink it is, whether one bowl or both are slow, and how long it has been that way
  • Whether a disposal or dishwasher is connected, and whether the dishwasher water shows in the sink
  • Whether other drains gurgle, run slow or smell
  • Any dampness under the cabinet or recent use of chemical drain opener
  • Whether you own the home or rent, since access to a shared stack may depend on the building
