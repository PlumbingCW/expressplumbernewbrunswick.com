Why Is My Tankless Water Heater Not Getting Hot in New Brunswick, NJ?

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so lukewarm output has a different set of causes than a tank heater. Some checks take two minutes, and some belong to a licensed plumber.

By the New Brunswick Express Plumbing team

(732) 466-1098

Lukewarm, cold or cycling: describe the symptom first

Tankless units fail in different ways, and the symptom narrows the cause fast. Water that is slightly warm all the time points one way. Water that goes hot, then cold, then hot again points another.

Take a minute to note which describes your house. Does one faucet fail while others work? Does the problem appear only when two fixtures run together? Does the unit show an error code? Those answers shape everything below.

Cause by cause: a quick decision table

Here are the usual reasons a tankless heater falls short, and what you can safely check yourself before calling.

SymptomCommon causeSafe first check
Warm but never hotFlow is too high for the unitRun one fixture at a time and compare
Hot, then cold, then hotFlow too low to trigger firingClean faucet aerators and check for a closed valve
Cold only at one faucetCross connection or a mixing valveTest other faucets and note the fixture
No hot water at allNo gas, no power or a tripped safetyCheck the display for a code and the power switch
Output drops over monthsScale buildup inside the heat exchangerAsk about descaling, see the flushing section

The table covers the common cases, not every case. Rental units and older homes sometimes have quirks that need an on-site look.

Demand: the unit may be asked to do too much

Every tankless heater has a maximum flow it can heat by a set number of degrees. Shower, dishwasher and washing machine running together can exceed that. The water then arrives lukewarm, not because the unit is broken, but because it is stretched.

Try running only the shower for a few minutes. If it heats well alone, you are likely hitting the unit's limit. A plumber can tell you whether the model fits your household or whether a second unit or a larger one makes sense.

Hard water, scale and the flush

Mineral buildup coats the inside of the heat exchanger and reduces how well it moves heat. The unit then struggles to hit its set temperature, especially at higher flow. Many manufacturers call for periodic flushing with a descaling solution.

We have not verified water hardness for New Brunswick, so ask your water utility for its annual water quality report and check the hardness figure. A plumber can then recommend how often to flush. See our water heater repair page for what service visits usually cover.

Cold incoming water makes the job harder

A tankless unit raises the temperature of whatever enters it. In the coldest weeks, incoming water is colder, so the same flow that gave a hot shower in October may feel weaker in January. That is physics, not always a fault.

Lower the flow a little, and the water comes out hotter. If it only fails in winter, tell the plumber. It may mean the unit sits close to its limit for your household, and sizing is worth a conversation.

Water quality is local, so ask for the report

Scale buildup depends on your water supply, and a water supplier publishes an annual report. We could not verify the supplier or hardness figure for New Brunswick for this post, so we are not stating one. Ask your water utility for the most recent report and look for the hardness number.

Bring that number to the plumber. It helps decide how often the unit should be flushed and whether a scale filter is worth discussing. Also tell the plumber the unit model, its age if you know it and any error codes you saw. Those details save time on the first visit.

Long pipe runs and the cold slug

Even a healthy heater cannot warm the pipe between it and a far bathroom. You get a cold slug first, then hot water. This feels like a failing heater, yet it is a layout issue.

If it takes a long time to reach a distant faucet, ask about a recirculation setup. Some units support one and some do not. Check your model before you buy parts for it.

Fuel, power and venting problems

Venting and gas problems are not homeowner repairs. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Gas supply

A closed gas valve, an empty propane cylinder or a low supply can keep the burner from firing.

Electrical

Tankless units need power for the controls. A tripped breaker or unplugged cord shuts everything down.

Venting

A blocked or damaged vent triggers a safety shutdown, and that is a hazard to take seriously.

Sensors

Faulty flow or temperature sensors give the unit bad data and it may fire at the wrong time.

Checks you can do yourself

  1. Read the displayNote any error code and the set temperature. Many units list code meanings in the manual.
  2. Check the shutoff valvesThe water and gas valves near the unit should be fully open, in line with the pipe.
  3. Clean faucet aeratorsA clogged screen can cut flow below the point where the unit fires.
  4. Reset the unit onceTurn it off at the switch, wait a minute and turn it on. Do not keep resetting it if it keeps failing.
  5. Stop at any gas smell or sootLeave the area and call the gas utility or 911. Do not try to relight anything.

Rentals, shared buildings and who to call

In a rental or a multifamily building, the heater may be the landlord's responsibility. We did not verify the state rule on hot water in rentals for this post, so check your lease and ask the landlord or building manager before arranging repairs.

If you own the home and the unit is still not hot after the basic checks, a plumber should look. Nearby towns such as Piscataway and East Brunswick get the same service from us, and our line is open 24/7 for no-hot-water calls.

If the fix means replacing the unit, a permit is involved. According to the state Department of Community Affairs code alert, replacing a water heater with a new one of like capacity is minor work that needs a permit through the local enforcement agency. Tenants should ask the landlord who handles that step.
