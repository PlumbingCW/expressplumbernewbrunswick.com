Water Meter Still Running With Everything Off in New Brunswick, NJ?

If the meter keeps moving after every tap is shut, water is going somewhere you cannot see. A short test tells you whether it is a real leak, and where to look next.

By the New Brunswick Express Plumbing team

(732) 466-1098

Run the two minute test first

  1. Shut every tapMake sure no one is showering, no washer or dishwasher is running, and the ice maker is not filling. Flush nothing.
  2. Find the meterIt is often in the basement near the front wall, or in a pit outside near the curb. Lift the lid with care and keep fingers clear.
  3. Read the leak indicatorMany meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves. If it turns with everything off, water is flowing.
  4. Write the numbersNote the reading, wait fifteen minutes with all water off, and read again. A change means a leak.
  5. Close the main valveTurn the main shutoff clockwise, then check the meter. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is before the valve, on the service line.

Common culprits when the meter will not stop

Running toilet

A worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl. A drop of food coloring in the tank will show up in the bowl in ten minutes if it leaks.

Dripping fixtures

A hot water tap that never quite closes can add up over a month.

Water heater relief valve

A weeping valve or discharge pipe near the heater is a small constant loss.

Buried line

A crack between the street and the house can leak into the soil without any wet spot showing.

A meter that moves in bursts is not always a leak. Some water treatment systems and ice makers refill on their own, so rule those out before calling anyone.

What to do before the next bill

Write down today's reading and the time. Photograph the meter. Repeat in the morning before anyone uses water. That record shows whether the loss is steady and gives your utility something concrete to look at.

Call the utility and ask how they handle an unexplained high bill. Some will review usage history, and some have rules for leak adjustments. We have not verified how New Brunswick handles that, so ask before assuming anything. Keep notes of who you spoke with and when.

Fix the cheap, visible things first. A toilet flapper or a faucet washer is quick, and if the meter still moves afterward you have narrowed the search.

Signs a leak is behind the walls or under the floor

  • A damp patch, stain or bubbling paint on a ceiling or wall.
  • A musty smell in a closet, basement corner or under a sink.
  • A warm spot on the floor, which can point to a hot water line.
  • Soft or lifting flooring near a bathroom or kitchen.
  • The sound of water hissing when the house is quiet.
  • Wet ground or a patch of grass that stays green near the street side of the house.

Why a small leak still matters

A leak that drips into a wall does not stay small. Moisture feeds mould, loosens flooring and softens drywall, and the meter is charging you for every gallon along the way. Finding it early is almost always the cheaper road, even when the repair itself takes effort.

Keep in mind that a slow leak can run for months before a stain appears. Your meter is the one honest witness, because it counts water whether or not you see it. Check it again after any repair to confirm the number has stopped changing.

Seasonal checks help too. Look at the meter before and after a vacation, and compare winter and summer readings, so you learn what normal looks like for your household.

How a plumber finds what you cannot see

Finding a hidden leak is a process of elimination. A plumber isolates sections of pipe with valves, listens with sensitive equipment, and may use thermal imaging or pressure tests to narrow down the area before cutting anything open. The goal is a small, precise opening, not a hole in every wall.

We cannot say in advance what a technician will find. Common results include a failed fitting, a pinhole in an older pipe, a leaking supply line to an appliance, or a break in the buried line. Our water line repair page explains what happens when the problem is outside the house.

Ask for the price in writing before work starts. It depends on how hard the leak is to reach, how much needs to be opened, and whether the line is inside or outside.
