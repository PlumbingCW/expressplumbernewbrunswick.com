No Hot Water? Safe Gas and Electric Heater Checks for New Brunswick, NJ Homes

A cold shower is a bad start to the day, and the fix may be a switch, a breaker or a pilot light. These checks cover both fuel types and tell you where to stop and call.

By the New Brunswick Express Plumbing team

(732) 466-1098

Smell gas or hear an alarm? Stop here

If you smell gas, leave the house at once, do not flip switches or use a phone inside, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone into fresh air and call 911. No hot water can wait. These cannot.

If water is pooling around the heater, shut the cold supply valve above it, and do not step into standing water near electrical items. For an electric unit, turn off its breaker only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor.

The shared problems matter most. A leaking base is a tank failure, not a settings issue, and a heater that has run out of hot water because everyone showered back to back may simply need time to recover.

Gas heater checks, one at a time

  1. Confirm the heater has a flameLook at the small window or status light on the control. If the pilot is out and you smell no gas, many units have printed relighting steps on the label. Follow them exactly or leave it to a plumber.
  2. Check the gas valve and dialThe gas shutoff handle on the pipe should run parallel to the pipe when open. The temperature dial should sit above the lowest setting, since someone may have turned it down.
  3. Listen for ignitionA click and a brief glow, then a flame, is normal. Repeated clicking with no flame, or a flame that goes out after a few seconds, often means a failing igniter or thermocouple.
  4. Look at the ventThe flue above the heater should be connected and free of debris, soot or loose joints. Staining or a damp, sooty smell means stop and call.
  5. Stop after one relight attemptIf the burner will not hold, do not keep trying. Repeated failed lighting is a sign of a parts problem or a safety shutdown doing its job.

Electric heater checks and when to stop

Breaker

Find the double breaker labeled for the heater. If it sits in the middle or off position, switch it fully off, then on. If it trips again, leave it off and call, because that points to a short or failed part.

Reset button

Behind a small access panel is a red reset button that trips on overheating. Pressing it means opening the panel, which should only happen with the breaker off, and the first press is the only one worth trying.

Elements and thermostats

Failed upper or lower elements give lukewarm water or short supply. Testing them needs a meter and live power at some stage, so this belongs to a plumber.

Tank state

Rumbling, popping or a very short supply can mean sediment has built up, which weakens heating and strains the unit over time.

Whatever the fuel, do not raise the thermostat to the top setting to chase hotter water. Very hot water scalds quickly, especially for children and older adults, and it wears the tank faster. If the dial is already at a sensible setting and the water is still cold, the cause lies elsewhere.

Also remember that a heater which has only just been repaired or reset needs time to reheat a full tank. Give it a reasonable wait before deciding the fix failed.

When repair stops making sense

Age and condition decide the next move. A heater that has leaked at the tank seam cannot be patched, and one that has needed several repairs in a short time is telling you its parts are wearing out together. In those cases water heater replacement is often the more sensible choice than another round of repairs.

Ask for the written price for each option before work starts, and ask what is included, such as old unit removal, venting and fittings. A clear comparison beats a rushed decision at a cold tap.

The size of the household matters too. If hot water runs out only when several people use it, the tank may be too small, not broken.

Before you agree to a new heater, know that a replacement of like capacity requires a permit, according to a state Department of Community Affairs code alert. A contractor who says no permit is needed is worth questioning.
