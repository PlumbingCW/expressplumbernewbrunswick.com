Emergency Sewer Line Repair in New Brunswick, NJ

When sewage comes up through a floor drain or nothing leaves the house, the problem is usually in the line outside. We help you pick between a spot repair, lining and full replacement, and we find out which one your pipe needs first.

(732) 466-1098

Sewage in the house or no drainage at all

If waste water is backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain, or nothing drains anywhere, treat it as urgent. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and while you wait you can stop running water, keep children and pets out of the wet area and open a window if the smell is strong.

Do not flush, do not run the washing machine and do not pour chemical drain opener into a line that is already full. Keep your hands off any electrical item or outlet near standing sewage, and shut off power to that area at the panel if it is safe to reach. A gas smell means leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

  • Stop all water use, including dishwashers and washers.
  • Note which drains back up first. The lowest drain in the house usually shows it.
  • Photograph the backup for your insurer before cleanup.
  • Call us and say whether one fixture or every fixture is affected.

Why a repair is not the same as a cleaning

A clog and a broken pipe look alike from inside the house. Both back up the lowest drain. The difference shows up when the line is cleared and the backup comes back within days or weeks.

Roots, a sagging section, a joint that has opened and a collapsed wall all keep catching waste after cleaning. That is when a camera run through the line, as described on our sewer inspection page, matters more than another round of snaking.

Repairing a sewer line is plumbing work, and according to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board, it may only be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Ask who will be on your property and who is pulling any permit.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

Spot repair, lining or replacement

OptionFits whenTrade-off
Spot repairOne broken or offset section, rest of pipe soundNeeds digging at that spot only
Trenchless liningPipe is intact but cracked, rooted or leaking at jointsNeeds a pipe that has not collapsed or bellied badly
Full replacementCollapsed, flattened, or failing along its lengthLargest job, longest site disruption

A technician can only recommend one of these after seeing the pipe on camera. Anyone who names the method over the phone is guessing.

What failing pipe looks like

Cast iron

Old cast iron rusts from the inside and builds rough scale that catches paper. Over time it can crack or flake apart.

  • Slow drains that return after cleaning
  • Rust-colored water or flakes in the cleanout

Clay

Clay pipe comes in short lengths with many joints. Roots find those joints and push them apart.

  • Repeated root blockages
  • Offset joints visible on camera

Orangeburg

Orangeburg is a bonded fiber pipe that softens and deforms with age. It tends to oval, flatten or collapse rather than crack.

  • Sudden drop in drain flow
  • A sag or flattened section on camera

We do not assume your pipe type from the age of the street. The camera tells us what you have.

Who owns which part of the line

Many properties have a private lateral running from the house to a sewer main in the street, and where private responsibility ends varies by municipality. Do not assume the rule. Ask the city or your own town's public works office who owns the section under the sidewalk and street, and whether a repair there needs their approval.

In Highland Park, the borough's sewer network is operated by a Middlesex Water subsidiary, so a main-line backup there goes to that operator. If you are across the line in Highland Park or North Brunswick, the same question applies to your own municipality.

Source: Middlesex Water, Utility Service Affiliates, Highland Park page.

Steps from first call to finished repair

  1. Call and describeTell dispatch which fixtures are backing up and whether any are still draining.
  2. Camera the lineWe run a camera from a cleanout to see the break, root mass or sag and note how far it is from the house.
  3. Choose the methodYou get the options from the table above with a clear price before work starts.
  4. Permit checkAsk the building department whether your repair needs a permit. We help you with that question when you call.
  5. Repair and retestAfter the repair we run water through the line and confirm it flows.

What drives the cost of a sewer line repair

Depth and locationA line under a driveway or beside a foundation takes more work than one in open yard.
Length of damageA single break costs less than a section that has failed end to end.
MethodLining, spot repair and replacement each use different crews and materials.
Surface restorationCutting through concrete or landscaping adds to the job.
Access and cleanoutsA missing cleanout may need to be added before the camera can enter.

Homeowners policies often cover sudden pipe failure but not wear, so ask your insurer before work starts. Many sewer backups from slow deterioration fall on the wear side.

We give you a real number by phone once we know what is going on, and a clear price before work starts. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.
