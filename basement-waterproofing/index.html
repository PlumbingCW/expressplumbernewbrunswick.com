Basement Waterproofing in New Brunswick, NJ

Before anyone digs or drills, the first job is working out where the water comes from. Some wet basements are plumbing, some are groundwater, and the fix is different for each.

(732) 466-1098

Is it a pipe or the ground?

Use the timing test before you hire anyone. If the floor or wall gets wet after rain or snowmelt and dries out in dry weather, the water is coming from the ground. If it appears when water is used in the house, or stays wet in dry weather, suspect a supply line, drain, water heater or appliance.

Plumbing causes can be fixed by a plumber. Groundwater entering through the foundation walls or floor slab often calls for a waterproofing or foundation contractor, and we will tell you plainly when that is the right call.

Condensation on cold pipes also looks like a leak. Wipe the pipe dry and see if it beads up again on humid days.

What a plumber can and cannot do

A plumber works on pipes, drains, sump pits and the pumps that move water out. That covers interior drain systems, sump installation, floor drain backflow protection and finding leaks in lines. It does not cover cracked foundation walls, structural movement or exterior membrane work.

If a crack is leaking or a wall is bowing, a foundation contractor or structural engineer is the right first call. We would rather say that than sell you a drain that does not fix the cause.

According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board, plumbing in New Jersey may only be performed by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. That matters here because sump lines and interior drain connections count as plumbing work, so ask who is actually doing the piping.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx

Interior drains and sump systems

Perimeter drain channel

A channel along the base of the wall catches water and carries it to a pit.

Sump pit and pump

Collects the water and pushes it out of the house. See sump pump installation and repair for the pump side.

Battery backup

Keeps a pump running when power fails during a storm.

Check valve and discharge

Stops pumped water flowing back and sends it away from the foundation.

Where the discharge pipe may run is a local question. Ask the building department or public works office whether your township limits sump discharge to the street or a neighbor's lot.

Local water and flooding questions

We do not have a sourced flood history for New Brunswick, so we will not describe one. You can look up whether your address is in a mapped flood zone on the FEMA flood map service, and ask the building department about past basement water in your area. Both answers change how much drainage work is worth doing.

In Highland Park, a Middlesex Water subsidiary operates the borough sewer network, so floor drain backups after storms there are worth reporting to that operator. In North Brunswick, ask the township how sump discharge is handled before routing a pipe.

Source: Middlesex Water, Utility Service Affiliates, Highland Park page.

Humidity and finishing a basement

A damp basement can feel wet without any leak. Keep indoor humidity moderate with a dehumidifier, vent dryers outside and do not store cardboard on bare floors.

Before finishing the space, fix the water source and run a moisture check on walls and floor. Rough-in plumbing for a new bathroom or laundry is a separate job, covered under plumbing installation. Ask the building department what permits a finished basement needs.
