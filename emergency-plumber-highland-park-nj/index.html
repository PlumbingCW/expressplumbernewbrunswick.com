Water is coming in and you are in Highland Park

This is urgent, and our phone line is open 24/7 for Highland Park, including nights, weekends and holidays. While you wait for a technician, shut the main water valve, switch off power to anything the water is near and move what you can off the floor.

If the water is rising from a floor drain or a toilet, the problem is probably the drain and not a supply pipe. Stop running water in the house and tell us that when you call. A gas smell means leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Before we arrive

Shut off the main valve

Find the main near where the line enters the house, often in the basement by the meter, and turn it clockwise until it stops. Then open the lowest tap to drain the pipes.

Power off wet areas

Use the breaker for the basement or laundry area if you can reach it dry. If the panel itself is wet, stay away and call the electric utility.

Clear the area

Lift boxes, rugs and cardboard off the floor and unplug anything that sits low, such as a dehumidifier or freezer. Never touch electrical items while standing in water.

Call the 24/7 line

Call (732) 466-1098 and say where the water shows, what you already shut off and whether a drain is involved. Ask us about the price before work begins.

Which pipe belongs to whom

Utility Service Affiliates, Inc., a subsidiary of Middlesex Water Company, operates the Borough's water and wastewater system, and all of the Borough's water is purchased from Middlesex Water. That means a bill question or a main break in the street goes to that operator, while a leak inside your walls is a plumbing call to us.

The system runs about 38 miles of water main and 32 miles of sewer main, so most homes sit close to shared lines. If water is bubbling in the street, report that to the operator. If it is inside, shut your own valve and call us. For hardness or lead figures, ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

New Jersey also has a lead service line law that took effect in July 2021, according to the state Department of Environmental Protection. It calls for all lead lines to be replaced by 2031, and water systems must notify customers. Ask your water operator what your line is made of.

Backups, floor drains and the sewer main

The Borough's wastewater system has more than 650 sanitary manholes, and the sewer main under the street is owned by the system, not by you. A way to tell the difference is simple. If only one fixture is slow, the clog is likely in your own line. If the lowest drain and every toilet back up together, the blockage may be further out.

For a blockage on your side, we start with sewer line cleaning and can send a camera down the line if it keeps coming back. If the backup is in the street main, you will need the operator to act, and we can tell you what we saw.

What the river next door shows

Highland Park neighbors New Brunswick, where Route 18 was partly submerged by the Raritan River during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 and a student tower basement flooded.

The useful lesson is general. If your basement has taken water once, plan before the next storm. Check the sump pit and discharge line, and look at basement waterproofing so water has a path out other than your floor.

Who can do the work and when a permit applies

New Jersey allows plumbing work only by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. A licensed plumber must display the plumbing license on vans, invoices and advertising, so you can check it.

Replacing a water heater with one of like capacity is classed as minor work and needs a permit through the town, while a like-for-like faucet or toilet swap needs none. Ask the Highland Park building department what applies to your job.
