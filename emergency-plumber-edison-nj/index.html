Burst pipe or flooding in Edison right now

If water is coming through a ceiling, a pipe has split or a drain is pushing sewage back up, call (732) 466-1098 now. Before we arrive

Shut the main valve

Find the main shutoff where the water line enters the house, often near the meter or water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Do this even if the leak looks small.

Power off wet areas

If water is near outlets, a furnace or a basement panel, switch off that circuit at the breaker only if you can reach it dry. Never touch electrical items while standing in water.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and stored items off the floor. In a multi-family building, warn the neighbors below so they can protect their own space.

Call the 24/7 line

Call (732) 466-1098. Tell us which fixture, where the water is going and whether the main is off. Photos of the damage help for insurance later.

Permits, licenses and what to ask a plumber

New Jersey allows plumbing work only by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Licensed plumbers must display their plumbing license marking on vehicles, invoices and advertising, so check the van and the invoice.

Replacing a water heater with a new one of like capacity is minor work. A permit is required, and work may begin once notice is given to the local enforcement agency. Swapping a fixture for a similar one with no piping change needs no permit. Ask Edison's building department how it handles each case.

Source: NJ Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ and NJ DCA code alert on the 2018 NJ Register changes.

Hard water, your bill and the water meter

Middlesex Water Company's 2025 water quality report gives an average hardness of 157 ppm across its whole system, with a range of 90 to 308 ppm. That report does not name Edison, so we cannot say your home is on that system.

Check your own water bill to see who supplies you. Then ask that utility for its annual water quality report, and test your tap. Where water is hard, scale builds up in water heaters and on fixtures, and a heater that has scaled up can leak sooner.

A bill that jumps with no change in use points to a hidden leak, and leak detection can trace it without opening every wall.

New Jersey's lead service line law took effect July 22, 2021 and calls for full removal by July 2031, according to the NJDEP. Systems must replace the line from the main to the building inlet, so ask your water system what your own line is made of.

Source: Middlesex Water Company 2025 Consumer Confidence Report, published 2026. Results are system-wide, Edison service area not verified. and NJDEP lead service line law webinar, nj.gov/dep/watersupply/pdf/njdep-lslr-law-webinar-presentation.pdf
