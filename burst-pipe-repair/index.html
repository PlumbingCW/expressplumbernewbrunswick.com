A split pipe can put hundreds of gallons into your walls fast. Call (732) 466-1098, shut the water off, and we will get a plumber moving on the repair.

Water is pouring from a pipe right now

This is urgent, and our dispatch line at (732) 466-1098 is open 24/7. While you wait for a plumber, stop the water at the source. Find the main shutoff, usually where the line enters the house near the meter or in the basement, and turn it clockwise until it stops.

Then open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain what is left in the pipes. If the ceiling is bulging or dripping near light fixtures, stay out of that room and switch off the breaker for it.

  • Never step into standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.
  • Photograph the damage before you clean up. Your insurer will ask for it.

Frozen pipes: thaw slowly and never with a flame

  1. Keep the tap openLeave the affected faucet open so melting water has somewhere to go and pressure does not build behind the ice.
  2. Warm the area, not the pipeA hair dryer on low or a space heater placed a safe distance away works. A torch or open flame can ignite framing and split the pipe.
  3. Work toward the blockageStart near the tap and move back along the pipe. Check for drips or wet spots as the ice clears.
  4. Stop and call if water appearsA leak on thaw means the pipe has already split. Close the main valve and call for a repair.

Pipes on outside walls, in crawlspaces and in unheated garages freeze first. Insulating them and letting a tap drip on a hard-freeze night is cheap protection.

Why pipes split in Middlesex County homes

We do not have a sourced water report for New Brunswick, so ask your water utility for its latest annual water quality report and read the pH line. Next door, the North Brunswick report lists a pH of 6.41, a little below the 6.5 to 8.5 range it cites. Slightly acidic water can eat at older copper and galvanized steel over decades.

Corrosion thins pipe walls from the inside, and the thin spot gives out under pressure or a hard freeze. If your plumber finds pitting in one run, the same wear is likely in the rest of that line. That is the point to ask about repiping instead of patching one split at a time.

Burst supply valves, connectors and the lines under sinks

Shutoff valves

Old multi-turn valves can crack at the stem or spray from the packing nut when turned. If a valve will not close, use the main.

Braided supply lines

The hose under a sink or behind a toilet can burst at the crimp. These are among the quickest floods because they run at full line pressure.

Rigid pipe joints

Soldered and threaded joints loosen with age and movement. A drip at a joint can become a spray without warning.

What the repair usually involves

Most bursts need the water off, the damaged section cut out, and new pipe joined in. Where the line is old and failing in several places, a longer replacement is common. Where the break is buried or in a wall, the plumber may open drywall to reach it.

If the failure is on the line between the street and the house, that is a different job, covered under water line repair. We tell you which situation you have before we start work.

After the water stops: photos, drying and your insurer

Take wide and close photos of the burst pipe, the wet walls and any damaged belongings before cleanup. Keep the failed section if the plumber removes it, since an insurer may want to see it.

Drying matters as much as the repair. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold within days, so ask your insurer or a water restoration company about extraction and drying. Run fans and open windows in the meantime if the weather allows.
