Water is spreading through your East Brunswick home

A pipe has split, a toilet is overflowing or the basement is taking on water, and you need someone now. Our dispatch line answers 24/7 for East Brunswick, so call first and keep the phone nearby. While you wait, close the main water valve by turning it clockwise until it stops.

Open one faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain what is left in the pipes. If water is near outlets, appliances or a breaker panel, switch off power to that area only if you can reach the switch without standing in water. A gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

  • Take photos of the water and the damage before you clean up.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the wet floor.
  • Write down where you first saw the water and when.

Before we arrive

Shut off the main valve

In most single-family homes it sits where the water line enters, often in a basement or utility room. Turn it clockwise. If it will not move, do not force it. Tell dispatch.

Power off wet areas

Flip the breaker for a flooded basement, laundry area or kitchen if the panel is dry and reachable. Never touch an outlet, cord or appliance while standing in water.

Clear the area

Lift stored items off the floor and clear a path to the water heater, the main valve and the wet fixture.

What an 80 percent owner-occupied town means for repairs

About 80.0 percent of East Brunswick housing units are owner-occupied, with 2.93 people per household on average. That points to mostly single-family homes where the owner holds the whole system, from the shutoff valve to the water heater to the drain line.

No landlord is coming to handle it, so the first call is usually yours. If you are unsure whether water is a leak behind a wall or a dripping fixture, describe it by phone and we will tell you what to check.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts, ACS 2020-2024 estimates

Failed water heater and the permit step

A tank that leaks at the base or leaves a household of three without hot water is often a replacement job. New Jersey classifies swapping a water heater for a new one of like capacity as minor work. A permit is required, and work may begin once notice is given to the local enforcement agency.

A licensed plumber pulls that permit, so you should not be asked to handle it yourself. For the full process, see our page on water heater replacement. Ask the East Brunswick building department what notice it wants for your address.

Source: NJ Department of Community Affairs code alert, March 5, 2018

Hard water, scale and what to ask your utility

Middlesex Water Company reported average hardness of 157 ppm in its 2025 water quality report, with a range of 90 to 308 ppm across its system. The report does not name East Brunswick, so we have not confirmed that it serves your street.

The practical step is simple. Find your water bill, see who the supplier is, and ask for the annual water quality report. Test your tap if scale keeps building on the water heater or fixtures. Hardness differs by supply source, so one number may not match your house.

Source: Middlesex Water Company Consumer Confidence Report, 2025 data, system-wide results

Water line, curb stop and who owns what

Ownership of the water service line depends on your utility. Where New Jersey American Water serves a home, the company owns the line from the main to the curb stop and the customer owns it from the curb stop to the building. The curb stop belongs to the company, so do not tamper with it. Call the utility.

A leak on your side of the curb stop is plumbing you pay for, and water line repair covers that work. New Jersey also requires water systems to replace lead service lines by July 2031, so ask your supplier what your line is made of.

Source: New Jersey American Water customer responsibilities page, NJDEP lead service line law presentation

Basement water and sump pumps

We have no confirmed East Brunswick flood record to cite. Nearby, New Brunswick basements flooded when the Raritan River overtopped Route 18 during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021. The lesson travels: a sump pump that has not been tested is a risk before the first heavy storm.

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch whether the pump starts and empties it. If it does not, or the basement is already wet, call us. We handle sump pump installation and repair and can discuss next steps by phone.

Source: Daily Targum, September 2021, and News 12, September 4, 2021

Hiring a plumber in New Jersey

New Jersey requires plumbing to be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Licensed plumbers must display their plumbing license on vans, invoices and advertising, so look for it before work begins. We are licensed and insured, and we give a clear price before work starts.

We also dispatch to Edison and North Brunswick, if a relative nearby needs help.

Before anyone opens a wall, confirm the person is a master plumber or works for an authorized plumbing contractor. According to the Consumer Affairs plumbing board FAQ, plumbing in New Jersey may only be performed by one of those, and W-2 employees of the contractor may do the work too.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ
