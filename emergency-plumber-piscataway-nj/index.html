Water is coming through a ceiling, floor or wall right now

Emergency dispatch runs around the clock in Piscataway, so call (732) 466-1098 whenever the problem starts. While you wait, turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops, then open one faucet on the lowest floor to drain the pipes.

Flip off the breaker for any room with water coming through the ceiling or pooling on the floor. Never touch a switch, outlet or appliance while you stand in water. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Busy households put a strain on water heaters and drains

The average Piscataway household has 2.95 people. More showers, dishwasher cycles and laundry loads mean a water heater that works harder and drains that carry more. A tank that has run daily for years can start to weep at the seams or the drain valve, and a slow tub drain often turns into a full stoppage.

A puddle around the base of the tank usually means the tank itself has failed, and a leak from the tank wall is not repairable. If so, a water heater replacement is the realistic fix, and we can talk through the options on the phone.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts, ACS 2020 to 2024

Permits when an emergency turns into a replacement

New Jersey law says plumbing work is to be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. A licensed plumber can pull the permit for you, and the state requires the plumbing license to be shown on the van and the invoice.

The state code alert says replacing a water heater with a new one of like capacity is minor work that needs a permit. Work may begin once notice goes to the local enforcement agency. Swapping a fixture for a similar one with no piping change needs no permit. Ask the Piscataway building department how it handles the notice.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board FAQ and NJ DCA code alert, March 2018

Old service lines and the lead replacement law

New Jersey's lead service line law took effect on July 22, 2021, and requires water systems to replace all lead lines by July 2031. The system must replace the full line from the main to the building. Customers are notified by mail until the work is done.

If a notice arrives, keep it and mention it when you call about a leak or low pressure on the incoming line.
