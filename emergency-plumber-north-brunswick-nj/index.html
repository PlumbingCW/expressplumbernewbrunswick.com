What the township water means for your pipes

The Township of North Brunswick Water Utility serves the town, with Veolia North America operating it under contract. Questions about taste, pressure or water quality go to the township.

The 2022 report lists hardness at 67.5 ppm as calcium carbonate, which is soft to moderately soft, so scale builds slowly in fixtures and water heaters. Its pH of 6.41 sits just under the recommended 6.5 to 8.5. Slightly acidic water can be hard on old copper and galvanized pipe, so watch very old lines for pinhole leaks.

Lead at the tap was low, with none of 31 samples over the 15 ppb action level. That says nothing about your own pipes, so if your home is older, ask what material your service line is made of.

Source: Township of North Brunswick Water Utility, 2022 water quality report (issued June 2023)

Before We Arrive

Shut off the main valve

Find the main shutoff where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, say so when you call.

Power off wet areas

Switch off breakers for the basement, laundry or kitchen if water is near outlets or appliances. Never touch a switch while standing in water.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. In a townhouse or condo, warn the neighbor, since water travels through shared walls.

Call the 24/7 line

Dial (732) 466-1098 with your address and what you have shut off. Stay clear of the wet area until help arrives.

Water lines, supply source and what breaks

The supply comes from the Delaware and Raritan Canal in Franklin Township, which the report says comes primarily from the Delaware River. That surface source is why the water is chlorinated, and tastes can shift with the seasons. A taste change is not a fault, but a pressure drop or a wet patch in the yard is.

If the ground is soggy between the street and the house, or your meter keeps moving with everything off, the leak may be on your side. That is a job for water line repair. Leave the curb valve alone and call the township about anything on its side.

Storm water and the basement

Next door, Route 18 in New Brunswick was partly under water when the Raritan River overflowed during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021, and basements flooded there too. Storms are a known risk in this part of Middlesex County, so a wet basement after a downpour gets a fast response.

If water rises from a floor drain, assume it is dirty and stay out of it. Do not run the washer or flush. A pump that fails in a storm is covered under sump pump installation and repair.

According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs plumbing board, state law limits plumbing work to a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. After a flood, that applies to repiping, drain repair and sump connections alike, so ask who is doing the work before you agree to it.

Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers FAQ, njconsumeraffairs.gov/plu/Pages/FAQ.aspx
